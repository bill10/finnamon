### Added
- **Fetch by AI can use the Chrome you already run.** `finnamon import --browser hsbc --attach [host:port]` opens one new tab at the bank in a Chrome started with `--remote-debugging-port=9222` (loopback only) instead of launching Finnamon's own window, and the dashboard's Fetch by AI does it by itself whenever a Chrome answers on that port. You log in yourself as before; the session is pinned to that one tab (no listing, opening or switching tabs) and closes it at the end. It runs the build and the device your bank already knows; the trade-off is a debugging port on your everyday profile while it runs.
- **Fetch without AI.** A button next to Fetch by AI (and `finnamon import --browser <bank> --no-cdp`) opens Finnamon's Chrome at the bank with no debugging port and no AI attached. It shows the export steps for the bank and imports the CSV you download as soon as it lands, after a preview you confirm. This is the path that works if the debugging port is what makes HSBC refuse the login.

### Fixed
- **HSBC's "reference: EAC" is explained on screen, as the possible causes it is.** Nobody outside HSBC knows exactly what EAC means. Three suspects, likeliest first: the debugging port Finnamon opens its window with, Finnamon's own profile reading as a new device, or a Chrome build the bank hasn't seen (Chrome on disk newer than your open windows). When the builds differ, `finnamon import --browser`, the dashboard's Import tab and `finnamon doctor` note both builds and all three suspects (the window still opens), and give the ways out: the CSV from your everyday browser, `--attach`, or retrying in a few days. `finnamon import --browser hsbc --diagnose` finds the cause by watching what happens: you log in by hand on Finnamon's profile without the port, then with it, and it says which suspect your answers point to (kept in `chrome-diagnose.json`).
