### Added
- **Fetch by AI can use the Chrome you already run.** `finnamon import --browser hsbc --attach [host:port]` opens one new tab at the bank in a Chrome started with `--remote-debugging-port=9222` (loopback only) instead of launching Finnamon's own window, and the dashboard's Fetch by AI does it by itself whenever a Chrome answers on that port. You log in yourself as before; the session is pinned to that one tab (no listing, opening or switching tabs) and closes it at the end. It runs the build and the device your bank already knows; the trade-off is a debugging port on your everyday profile while it runs.
- **Fetch without AI.** A button next to Fetch by AI (and `finnamon import --browser <bank> --no-cdp`) opens Finnamon's Chrome at the bank with no debugging port and no AI attached. It shows the export steps for the bank and imports the CSV you download as soon as it lands, after a preview you confirm. Nothing attaches to the browser at any point.
- **Reset browser profile.** A button in the Import CSV window (and `finnamon import --browser --reset-profile`) moves Finnamon's Chrome profile aside and starts a fresh one, since HSBC may remember a profile that once got "reference: EAC" as a bad device.

### Fixed
- **HSBC's "reference: EAC" is explained, with what is known about it.** The import session (`finnamon import --chrome-check`), the docs and the messages say what is left after the owner's tests ruled out the Chrome build, a new device and an open debugging port: Finnamon's own browser profile, or a DevTools client attached during the login. They give the ways out: Fetch without AI, Reset browser profile, or the CSV from your everyday browser. `finnamon import --browser hsbc --diagnose` finds the cause by watching what happens. You log in by hand three times: on Finnamon's profile without the port, then with it, then on a throwaway profile with a DevTools client attached. It says which cause your answers point to and keeps them in `chrome-diagnose.json`.
