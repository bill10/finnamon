### Added
- **Fetch by AI can use the Chrome you already run.** `finnamon import --browser hsbc --attach [host:port]` opens one new tab at the bank in a Chrome started with `--remote-debugging-port=9222` (loopback only) instead of launching Finnamon's own window, and the dashboard's Fetch by AI does it by itself whenever a Chrome answers on that port. You log in yourself as before; the session is pinned to that one tab (no listing, opening or switching tabs) and closes it at the end. It runs the build and the device your bank already knows; the trade-off is a debugging port on your everyday profile while it runs.
- **Fetch without AI.** A button next to Fetch by AI (and `finnamon import --browser <bank> --no-cdp`) opens Finnamon's Chrome at the bank with no debugging port and no AI attached. It shows the export steps for the bank and imports the CSV you download as soon as it lands, after a preview you confirm. Nothing attaches to the browser at any point.
- **Reset browser profile.** A button in the Import CSV window (and `finnamon import --browser --reset-profile`) moves Finnamon's Chrome profile aside and starts a fresh one, for when a profile itself goes bad.

### Fixed
- **Finnamon's Chrome now starts as if you had opened it.** On a Mac every browser fetch opens Chrome through LaunchServices (`open -na … --args`) with `--no-first-run`, instead of as a child of the dashboard's background job with its stripped environment. That launch was the one difference left when HSBC refused Fetch by AI's login ("reference: EAC") while the same Chrome opened by hand passed. Whether this fixes EAC is not confirmed yet.
- **HSBC's "reference: EAC" is explained, with what is known about it.** The import session (`finnamon import --chrome-check`), the docs and the messages say what the owner's tests ruled out: the build, a new device, an open port, the profile and an early attach. They give the ways out: Fetch without AI, or the CSV from your everyday browser. `finnamon import --browser hsbc --diagnose` compares launch methods by watching what happens. You log in by hand on three throwaway profiles, with Chrome started three ways, and it records each launch's method and environment in `chrome-diagnose.json`.
